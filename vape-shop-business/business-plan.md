# Immediate Priorities Briefing (Read This First)

## Your current status, as I understand it

You formed a Texas LLC that has been **administratively forfeited** (most likely a "tax forfeiture" under Tax Code Chapter 171 for missing franchise tax filings — this is the most common cause). You are reinstating it now. Until reinstatement is complete, the entity **cannot legally transact business, sign enforceable contracts, or maintain its liability shield** — and critically, officers/managers can become **personally liable** for debts the entity incurs while forfeited (Tex. Tax Code §171.255). That last point matters enormously for someone planning to fund a launch on personal credit cards.

## The exact reinstatement process (tax forfeiture path)

Source: [Texas SOS Terminations & Reinstatements FAQs](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/termreinfaqs.shtml), [Texas Comptroller — Reinstating or Terminating a Business](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/reinstate-terminate.php), [SOS Form 801 instructions](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/instructions/801.shtml).

| Step | What you do | Cost | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | File **all delinquent franchise tax reports** and Public Information Reports (PIRs) with the Comptroller via Webfile | Any tax + **$50 late filing penalty per late report** + penalty/interest on tax due. If revenue was under the no-tax-due threshold (~$2.47M), tax owed is likely $0, but reports must still be filed | Same day online |
| 2 | Pay all tax, penalty, and interest shown due | Varies — likely just penalties if dormant | Same day |
| 3 | Request a **Tax Clearance Letter (Form 05-377)** via Webfile or by submitting **Form 05-391** | Free | **2–3 business days** typical (Comptroller's stated timeline); longer by mail |
| 4 | File **SOS Form 801** (Application for Reinstatement and Request to Set Aside Tax Forfeiture) with the Tax Clearance Letter attached, via SOSDirect/SOSUpload | **$75** filing fee | ~3–10 business days online **[EST]** — SOSDirect filings are usually processed in a few days; expedited handling ($25) available |

**Realistic total: $125–$300 and 2–4 weeks** if only reports are missing, assuming no back taxes. **[EST]**

Two caveats:
- If the forfeiture was by the SOS for a different reason (e.g., failure to maintain a registered agent), the form and path differ (Form 811). Pull your entity's status on **SOSDirect** or call SOS (512-463-5555) to confirm the forfeiture type before filing anything.
- If the LLC name was taken by someone else while forfeited, you must reinstate under a new name. Check name availability on SOSDirect first.

## Do NOT do these until reinstatement is complete

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Sign a commercial lease in the LLC's name | Contract is voidable/unenforceable protection-wise, and you're personally liable anyway under §171.255. Landlords will also run an SOS status check and reject a forfeited entity |
| Apply for Comptroller tobacco/e-cigarette permits | Permit applications require an active taxpayer account in good standing |
| Open (or reactivate) a business bank account | Banks verify SOS status; a forfeited entity gets declined, and a declined application creates a record you don't want at banks already skittish about vape businesses |
| Take on debt "for the business" | Any liability incurred while forfeited can attach to you personally even after reinstatement |

# 2. Company Description

## 2.1 Legal structure and reinstatement roadmap

- **Structure: single-member Texas LLC** (your existing entity, reinstated), taxed as a sole proprietorship initially; revisit S-corp election with a CPA once net profit clears roughly $50–60K/yr **[EST]** (self-employment tax savings vs. payroll admin costs).
- **Why not the alternatives:** A corporation adds formality without benefit at this size. Forming a *new* LLC instead of reinstating avoids back penalties but loses entity history, may complicate the name, and Comptroller penalties for a dormant entity are usually small — reinstating is normally cheaper and faster. If back liabilities turn out to be large, get a payoff figure first, then compare against a fresh entity.
- Reinstatement steps, costs, and timeline: see the Immediate Priorities briefing above. **Risk of delay:** every week of delay pushes the permit → lease → build-out chain (Section 9); worse, anything signed pre-reinstatement is personal liability.

## 2.2 Ownership

100% owned by you (single member). If you later take an investor, do it via a properly papered membership-interest sale with a company agreement — never a handshake percentage. Keep 100% through opening if at all possible; a vape shop's valuation pre-revenue is effectively zero, so early equity sales are maximally dilutive.

## 2.3 Location strategy — recommended corridors

Context every broker will confirm: **Houston has no conventional zoning**, so vape retail siting is governed by deed restrictions, landlord policy, and state law rather than a zoning map. New state laws effective 9/1/2025 matter here: **SB 1316** bans e-cigarette *advertising* within 1,000 ft of a school or church, and **SB 1315** authorizes cities/counties to restrict tobacco/e-cig *sales* near schools, churches, and hospitals ([Click2Houston legislative summary](https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2025/02/28/series-of-texas-bills-aim-to-tighten-regulations-on-tobacco-e-cigarettes/)). Some Houston-area suburbs have gone further — **Sugar Land banned new smoke/vape shops outright** ([Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/fort-bend/2024/04/04/482619/sugar-land-bans-new-smoke-shops-vape-stores-and-hookah-bars/)). Practical rule: **site nothing within 1,000 ft of a school or church**, and stay inside Houston city limits where no shop-specific ban currently exists — but verify the current ordinance status the week you sign, because this is moving fast.

Rent benchmark: Houston metro average retail asking rent was **$21.28/SF NNN in Q1 2026**, with the Inner Loop averaging **$32.01/SF** and metro vacancy at a tight 5.5% ([Partners Real Estate Q1 2026 Houston Retail report](https://partnersrealestate.com/research/houston-retail-q1-2026-quarterly-market-report/)).

## 2.4 Store size

**1,000–1,200 SF.** Reasoning: the 5-minute format needs ~600 SF of sales floor (three product zones + queue), ~200 SF secure stockroom, ~100 SF office/break, plus circulation. Bigger invites browsing-lounge drift and higher rent/build-out; smaller can't hold backstock for high-turn SKUs, forcing stockouts that break the "always have my product" promise. **Why not the alternatives:** 1,500+ SF only makes sense with a lounge/hookah component (different business, different regulatory posture — Houston's smoking ordinance restricts indoor smoking); under 800 SF works as a kiosk model but kills backstock depth.

## 2.5 Lease considerations specific to vape/tobacco tenants

- **Landlord restrictions:** many centers (especially institutionally owned or grocery-anchored) prohibit tobacco/vape uses outright or have exclusive-use clauses protecting an existing tenant. Ask the broker to pre-screen — don't waste tours.
- **Use clause:** get it written broadly — "sale of vapor products, tobacco products, smoking accessories, and related convenience merchandise." A narrow clause becomes a problem if regulation forces product-mix shifts.
- **Personal guarantee:** assume it will be required for a new LLC. Negotiate a **burn-off** (guarantee ends or steps down after 12–24 months of clean payment) and cap it at 6–12 months' rent. Never sign an uncapped full-term PG on a first store.
- **Insurance rider:** landlord will require GL certificate; some insurers exclude vape — line up the policy quote *before* lease signing (Section 7.1).
- **Regulatory out-clause (push hard for this):** a termination or rent-abatement right if state/federal law prohibits the sale of products constituting a majority of revenue. Many landlords will refuse; even a diluted version has real option value.
- **Tenant improvement (TI) allowance:** in a soft center you may get $5–15/SF **[EST]**; in a hot corridor, likely nothing. Every TI dollar is a credit-card dollar you don't borrow.

## 2.6 Store concept and layout — the 5-minute floor plan

Concept: **convenience-store logic, vape-shop inventory.** Everything about the room says "grab and go," not "hang out."

```
 ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  STOCKROOM (secure, steel door)   │  OFFICE   │
 │───────────────────────────────────┴───────────│
 │   [ZONE C — wall: mods, tanks, e-liquid       │
 │    library, alphabetized salts]               │
 │                                               │
 │   [ZONE B — center gondola, low-profile:      │
 │    pods & coils by system, priced shelf tags] │
 │                                               │
 │  CHECKOUT COUNTER ══════════════╗             │
 │  (ID scanner, dual POS,         ║  [ZONE A —  │
 │   behind-counter disposables    ║   express   │
 │   on backlit menu board)        ║   wall:     │
 │            queue rail ─────────►║   top-50    │
 │                                 ║   sellers]  │
 │              ENTRY ►►───────────╝             │
 └───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Entry flow:** door opens onto **Zone A, the "express wall"** — the top ~50 SKUs (best-selling disposables/pods) at eye level with big price tags. A repeat customer's product is visible within 3 seconds of entering.
- **Menu board behind counter:** backlit, category-organized, mirrors the express wall — customers in line can order verbally ("Blue one, 5%") without touching shelves. Age-restricted items like disposables stay behind the counter, which also serves loss prevention and compliance.
- **Zone B:** center low gondola (max 54" high for sightlines): pod systems, coils, chargers — organized *by device system*, not by brand, so replenishment purchases are one-stop.
- **Zone C:** back wall for the browsing minority: mods, tanks, e-liquid/salt library alphabetized by brand with shelf-edge flavor tags.
- **Checkout:** counter within 15 ft of the door; **two POS lanes** (second opens at rush); ID scanner at each lane; contactless payment terminals customer-facing. Queue rail keeps the line off the express wall.
- **Signage:** category blade signs from the ceiling readable from the door; price on every facing (hunting for a price is the #1 hidden time cost in vape shops).
- **Lighting:** bright, retail-white (4000K) — signals "efficient store," discourages loitering, helps cameras.
- **Tech integration:** cameras on every zone + counter + door (cloud DVR); door chime; electronic article surveillance is unnecessary if high-value items are behind glass/counter.
- **The 5-minute test:** every future decision — new fixture, new SKU, new promo — gets one question: *does this make the 5-minute promise easier or harder to keep?*

# 3. Market Analysis

## 3.1 Market size and trend

- **National:** The US e-cigarette/vape market is generally estimated in the **$8–11B+ annual retail range and growing single-to-low-double digits**, with disposables the dominant format since 2021. (Directional figure — commonly cited by Grand View Research / Statista-type market reports; exact size varies by methodology. **[EST]**)
- **Adult usage base:** CDC/NHIS data has put adult e-cigarette use around **4–6% of US adults**, skewing heavily 18–34.
- **Houston:** ~2.3M city / ~7.5M metro population. Applying 4–6% adult vaping prevalence to Harris County's ~3.5M adults implies roughly **140K–210K adult vapers in the county [EST — derived, not sourced]**. A daily disposable user spends roughly $60–120/month **[EST]**. You need only ~600–900 regulars to hit the Section 7 base case — a tiny share of that pool.
- **No reliable Houston-specific vape market-size report exists publicly.** Anyone who hands you a precise "$XXM Houston vape market" number made it up. Validate demand the cheap way: transaction counts observed at competitor stores (sit in the parking lot and count for an hour at peak — seriously).

## 3.2 Target customer demographics

21–45, skewing male but less than historically; mixed income ($30K–$90K household); disproportionately shift workers, service industry, trades, and office commuters; buying frequency weekly-or-better. The unifying trait for our format is not demographics — it's **habit and time-sensitivity**: they know what they want and resent stores that slow them down.

## 3.4 Personas

## 3.5 Regulatory environment (the part most competitors get wrong)

- **Age:** Texas + federal law: **21 minimum** (Texas SB 21, 2019; federal T21), narrow military exception in Texas. Card **everyone under ~40** by policy; FDA conducts undercover buys and publishes retailer warning letters/civil money penalties.
- **Permits (Texas Comptroller — not TABC):** You need BOTH a **Cigarette/Cigar/Tobacco Products Retailer Permit ($180)** and an **E-Cigarette Retailer Permit ($180 standalone, or $90 if you hold the tobacco permit)**; each is per-location, runs on a two-year cycle ending May 31 of even years ([Comptroller tobacco permits](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/tobacco/tobacco-retailer-permit.php), [e-cig permit](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/tobacco/e-cig-retailer-permit.php)). Timing note: the current cycle began **June 1, 2026** — you'd buy a full two-year term. Also needed: **Texas Sales & Use Tax Permit** (free), City of Houston sign permit and Certificate of Occupancy for the space.
- **FDA deeming/PMTA — the structural risk:** vapor products require FDA marketing authorization. Only a few dozen products (largely tobacco- and menthol-flavored from major manufacturers, e.g., Vuse, NJOY, JUUL menthol) hold marketing orders. **The majority of flavored disposables sold in US stores are unauthorized and on the market only via enforcement discretion/backlog.** FDA has escalated import refusals and retailer warning letters. Stock accordingly: build the mix so the store survives if unauthorized disposables disappear (Section 4.2).
- **Texas SB 2024 (eff. 9/1/2025):** bans marketing/sale of vapes containing cannabinoids, kratom, kava, mushrooms, tianeptine, etc.; bans child-appealing designs/packaging; bans products **made in or marketed as from China** or other "foreign adversary" countries; violations are a **Class A misdemeanor** ([Vicente LLP summary](https://vicentellp.com/insights/texas-sb-2024-statewide-ban-on-hemp-derived-vapes-takes-effect-september-2025/)). Since a large share of disposables are Chinese-made, this reshapes what you can legally stock — buy only from distributors who provide country-of-origin and compliance documentation in writing.
- **SB 1313/1314/1315/1316 (eff. 9/1/2025):** child-appealing marketing ban; e-cig definition expanded to nicotine-free products; **local governments empowered to restrict sales near schools/churches/hospitals**; **e-cig ads banned within 1,000 ft of schools/churches** ([Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2025/02/28/series-of-texas-bills-aim-to-tighten-regulations-on-tobacco-e-cigarettes/)).
- **Houston/Harris County:** Houston's smoking ordinance (Code ch. 21, art. IX) bans smoking in enclosed public places — no in-store sampling/lounge. No Houston citywide vape-shop location ban as of this writing, but SB 1315 gives the city the tool; monitor City Council agendas. Suburbs like **Sugar Land already ban new shops**.
- **Advertising:** FDA requires the nicotine addictiveness warning on advertising for covered products; Texas restricts outdoor ads near schools/churches (above); most paid digital channels (Google/Meta) prohibit nicotine ads outright — plan marketing accordingly (Section 6).
- **What to watch:** a Texas flavor restriction or a state "registry" bill (several states now maintain lists of permitted vape products — Texas considered similar; a registry would instantly criminalize much of the disposable wall); FDA menthol/flavor rulemaking; Houston local ordinance under SB 1315.

## 4.2 Inventory strategy

- Launch with **~250 SKUs**, of which the top 50 ("express wall") should target ~70% of revenue. Reorder twice weekly; never stock out of a top-50 SKU — that's the brand promise.
- **Regulatory hedging rule:** keep unauthorized-flavored-disposable exposure below ~50% of inventory dollars, and keep FDA-authorized products (Vuse/NJOY/etc.) plus US-made e-liquid plus pouches as a permanent 35–40% of the wall. If enforcement removes gray-zone disposables, the store limps instead of dies.
- 30-day supply on A items, 21-day on B, no C items until data earns them. Dead SKU rule: no sale in 45 days → clearance and delist.

## 4.3 Pricing strategy

- Price **5–10% below gas stations** on matching SKUs (they're the true competitor for the quick trip), **at parity with dedicated vape shops** on the express wall, and take full margin on accessories/e-liquid where comparison shopping is weak.
- Everyday pricing, no couponing circus — speed positioning is cheapened by haggling. One standing mechanic: loyalty (Section 5.5) and a simple multi-buy on disposables ("2 for $X") that raises ticket size without slowing checkout.

## 4.4 Low-friction add-ons

Counter placement only, under $10, no explanation needed: USB-C cables/chargers, battery cases, lighters, gum/mints. Later: simple branded merch if the mascot brand (Section 6.4) earns affection. Nothing at the counter that generates questions.

## 7.3 The credit-card funding section (read twice)

The stated intent is a launch funded primarily on credit cards — and as of now, those cards don't exist yet (see Section 7.0 for why that means a 6–12 month capital-formation phase, not a card-application spree). This section governs how the card portion of the stack is used *when* it exists. Here is the unvarnished version.

**The math that kills stores:** $100K at 26% APR = **$2,167/month in interest alone** — before a dollar of principal, rent, or inventory. That is close to the entire base-case pre-debt profit in Months 4–8. Minimum payments (~2–3% of balance ≈ $2,500–3,000/mo) are designed to keep you in debt, not get you out. If revenue ramps slower than plan (it usually does), the balance compounds while the store is still cash-hungry — that's the debt spiral: borrowing on Card B to pay Card A's minimum. Once you're there, the store may be operationally profitable and still bankrupt you personally.

**Personal liability is total.** These are consumer cards: the LLC does not shield you (and anything charged while the LLC was forfeited is statutorily personal anyway). A failed store means the debt follows you at 26% while you job-hunt.

**If you proceed anyway, the risk-reduction playbook:**

1. **0% intro APR laddering, done deliberately:** open the 0% cards (12–21 month promos) *before* the LLC/lease events ding your credit and utilization; map every promo end date on one page; treat each promo end as a hard debt maturity with a payoff plan, like a bond ladder. Balance-transfer offers (3–5% fee) are your refinancing tool — model the fee as ~APR equivalent.
2. **Hard cap rule:** total card exposure ≤ a number you could service from a day job if the store died tomorrow. For most people that's $40–60K, not $118K. If the gap can't be closed with savings/microloan/family note, **build the lean version ($95K) or delay** — the lean store with a real cash buffer outlives the fancy store with none.
3. **Never put recurring fixed costs (rent, payroll, insurance) on cards.** Cards fund one-time capex and initial inventory only. The month rent goes on a card is the month the plan has failed — treat it as a tripwire to cut costs, not a bridge.
4. **Keep a cash buffer of ≥ 2 months' opex in a bank account at all times**, even while paying card interest. Illiquidity, not interest, is what actually closes doors.
5. **Pay down before promos end, refinance the rest:** at Month 10–12, with statements in hand, refinance remaining balances into a term loan/LOC (odds improve dramatically with 12 months of clean revenue).
6. **Weekly debt dashboard:** one page — each card, balance, APR, promo end, minimum. You cannot manage what you look at monthly.

**And the tripwire (decide it now, in writing):** if by Month 6 revenue is below ~60% of base case AND card balances are growing month-over-month, you cut to skeleton (owner-only staffing, inventory drawdown) or wind down. Deciding this now, calmly, is worth more than any projection in this document.

# 11. Website

Built and included in this repo at **`vape-shop-business/website/index.html`** — a single-file, working site (open it in any browser; no external dependencies). Uses the **Dash Vapor Co.** placeholder brand — the name appears in a handful of clearly marked places and is a find-and-replace away from whatever you choose.

Implemented per spec: SpaceX-style dark/high-contrast design with bold typography and scroll-triggered reveals; the **animated cigar mascot** (eyes, legs, feet, walk cycle) strolls along the bottom of the viewport as you scroll; an **ambient smoke system** drifts through every section; sections for Home, Products, About/Quick-Shopping concept, Hours & Location (placeholder, labeled), and Contact; and the **mandatory age gate** — full-screen on entry with its own floating smoke backdrop, "Yes" reveals the site, **"No" hard-locks the page** with no UI path back short of reloading.

**Update (owner decision):** the site now includes an **order-ahead system for in-store and curbside pickup** — cart, checkout with pickup-method choice, and order confirmation. It is deliberately structured as **reserve online, pay at pickup**: no money changes hands on the website, and nothing ships.

**Age-gating and online-ordering legal notes you should know:** (1) A click-through self-attestation gate is standard industry practice, but it is **not** a legal substitute for ID verification at the time of sale — on every pickup order (in-store *and* curbside), the ID gets scanned at handoff before payment; the gate protects brand posture, the scanner protects the license. (2) **Why pay-at-pickup instead of online payment:** mainstream gateways (Square online, Stripe, PayPal) prohibit vape/tobacco e-commerce, and online card-not-present vape processing is expensive high-risk territory. Taking payment at the counter/curb runs everything through your normal in-store POS. Revisit online payment only via a vape-friendly high-risk gateway, if order volume ever justifies the cost. (3) **Why pickup-only, never shipping:** shipping vapes triggers PACT Act registration, carrier bans, adult-signature delivery, and state tax filings — a different, much heavier business. Pickup orders are fulfilled at your permitted place of business; curbside handoff on the premises with ID scan is the model shops have used since COVID, but confirm with your regulatory attorney that curbside handoff at your specific site satisfies the Comptroller's "place of business" rules before launch. (4) Orders in the demo are not transmitted anywhere yet — before launch, connect the order form to your POS's e-commerce module or at minimum a store email/SMS flow, and turn on real inventory sync so the site never sells what the shelf doesn't have. (5) The FDA-style nicotine warning banner stays on every page; keep it when editing. (6) Don't run paid ads to the site (platforms prohibit nicotine promotion) and keep the URL off physical ads within 1,000 ft of schools/churches (SB 1316).
